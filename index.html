Confirmed without the nudge: keeping rides confirmed after the CCPA order

The consumer regulator has ordered Rapido to remove pre-ride tips, "add ₹10 to get a ride" prompts and the tilted price slider. Those prompts were how many unaccepted requests got accepted. This is a plan for the Customer Team to keep requests turning into rides without them, and without pressuring the customer.

Built for: Product Manager, Customer Team, Rapido (Bengaluru) Inside: the order · problem statement · metric · rules · root causes · 4 changes · screens · calculator · experiments · SQL · field plan Jump to the calculator

Independent work. Not affiliated with Rapido; no internal data used. All facts are from public reporting and are cited. The screens are my concept mock-ups, not Rapido's app. Calculator inputs are assumptions to be replaced with real data. I am not a lawyer: every change here would go through Rapido's legal review.

What changed

On 31 Aug 2026 the CCPA fined Rapido ₹10 lakh and ruled that pre-ride tips, "Captains aren't accepting at ₹60. Try adding +10, +20, +30", and a price slider coloured to push fares up are dark patterns. Tips are allowed only after the ride.

Why it's hard

Under the zero-commission model, captains see a request and choose. When the quoted fare is below what nearby captains will accept, the request just sits. The nudge was how the price found its level. Banning the nudge doesn't remove that gap.

The plan

Price right the first time, using the price data the old nudges already collected. Give an honest "still looking" screen with real alternatives. Make captains decide before accepting, not after. Move tips to after the ride.

2. The problem statement

Rapido must remove pre-ride tips, "add ₹X" prompts and the tilted price slider from booking. Those prompts got many otherwise-unaccepted requests accepted. Design a compliant booking flow that keeps the share of booking attempts that become completed rides at least where it is today, without pressuring the customer, and without hurting captain earnings.

Why the nudge existed (my reading, to verify with data)

  • Under subscription pricing, Rapido doesn't earn per ride, and captains choose which requests to take. A request quoted below what nearby captains will accept simply waits.
  • The "+₹10" prompt moved the price up until someone accepted. It worked as price discovery, but it put the pressure on the customer, after they had already decided to ride.
  • Remove the prompt and the gap is still there. Without a replacement, those requests become "no captain found", retries, or a switch to another app.

The hidden asset: every request that was accepted only after a top-up is a record of the price captains would actually accept, for that distance, time and area. Rapido has months of that data. It can be used to set the right price up front.

3. The metric: request-to-ride rate

Request-to-ride rate = completed rides ÷ booking intents. A booking intent is one customer trying to get from A to B: repeated attempts within 10 minutes count once.

  • Why count intents, not attempts: when a request fails, people retry, often at a higher price. Counting each attempt makes failure look smaller and the nudge look better than it was.
  • Break it down: request-to-ride = acceptance rate × (1 − cancellation after acceptance) × completion. Each part has a different owner and a different fix.
  • Also track: time to acceptance (p50 and p90), and wait from acceptance to pickup.
  • Guardrails: average fare per km (the new flow must not quietly raise prices), captain earnings per online hour (supply must stay healthy), complaints about extra fare or cash demands, and a compliance check: zero pre-ride tip or top-up prompts shown.

6. Four changes

1

One honest price, right the first time

For segments where requests were routinely accepted only after a top-up, set the upfront fare closer to the price captains actually accepted, staying within the MVAG band and showing a clear "high demand" note when it applies. The customer sees one price, decided before they book. No prompts later. This replaces pressure with accurate pricing, using data Rapido already has.

2

An honest "still looking" screen

If no captain has accepted after about 30 to 45 seconds, show neutral, useful choices with equal visual weight, ordered by expected wait: keep waiting (with a real time estimate), switch to another vehicle (with its real fare and time), move the pickup to a nearby main road where captains accept more often, or cancel for free. No colour that pushes the pricier option, no claims about chances unless they come from real data.

3

Captains decide before accepting, not after

Show captains the drop area and payment mode before they accept, so they skip instead of accepting and cancelling later. A customer gets a quick "no captain yet" instead of an 8-minute wait ending in a cancellation. After acceptance, an unjustified captain cancellation triggers the MVAG penalty, an automatic priority re-match, and compensation paid in money, not coins.

4

Tips after the ride, and only then

A simple optional tip screen at the end of the trip, with 100% going to the captain. Captains keep a way to earn more for good service; customers tip for service they've had, not to buy a ride.

The trade-off I'd accept: change 3 will lower raw acceptance a little, because captains can now skip trips they would have cancelled. That's the point: a fast "no" beats a slow "yes" followed by a cancellation. The request-to-ride rate is what decides whether it's worth it.

9. How I'd test it

  • Change 2 (the "still looking" screen): a normal user-level A/B test is fine. It changes what one customer sees and barely affects captain supply.
  • Changes 1 and 3 (pricing, what captains see): these change captain behaviour, and captains serve both groups. A user-level test would leak between groups. Use a switchback: each zone flips between old and new in 30 to 60 minute windows, balanced across hours and days, ignoring the first 10 minutes after each switch.
  • Removing the nudge itself is not an experiment. It's a legal requirement and ships everywhere. What gets tested is the replacements.
  • Primary: request-to-ride rate per intent. Guardrails: fare per km, captain earnings per online hour, extra-fare complaints, compliance count of zero.

10. SQL

1. The funnel per booking intent (retries within 10 minutes count once) 
WITH attempts AS (
  SELECT r.*,
         LAG(r.requested_at) OVER (PARTITION BY r.customer_id ORDER BY r.requested_at) AS prev_at
  FROM ride_requests r
  WHERE r.requested_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 14
),
marked AS (
  SELECT *,
         CASE WHEN prev_at IS NULL OR requested_at > prev_at + INTERVAL '10 minutes' THEN 1 ELSE 0 END AS new_intent
  FROM attempts
),
intents AS (
  SELECT *,
         SUM(new_intent) OVER (PARTITION BY customer_id ORDER BY requested_at) AS intent_no
  FROM marked
),
per_intent AS (
  SELECT customer_id, intent_no,
         MIN(city)          AS city,
         MIN(vehicle_type)  AS first_vehicle,
         COUNT(*)           AS attempts,
         BOOL_OR(accepted_at IS NOT NULL)          AS accepted,
         BOOL_OR(status = 'CANCELLED_BY_CAPTAIN')  AS captain_cancelled,
         BOOL_OR(status = 'COMPLETED')             AS completed
  FROM intents
  GROUP BY customer_id, intent_no
)
SELECT city, first_vehicle,
       COUNT(*)                                                     AS intents,
       ROUND(AVG(attempts), 2)                                      AS attempts_per_intent,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN accepted THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1)  AS accepted_pct,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN captain_cancelled THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1) AS captain_cancel_pct,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN completed THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1) AS request_to_ride_pct
FROM per_intent
GROUP BY city, first_vehicle
ORDER BY request_to_ride_pct;
2. The price captains actually accepted, from past top-ups (input to change 1) 
-- Every request accepted only after a top-up shows the price captains would take.
WITH nudged AS (
  SELECT city, vehicle_type,
         CASE WHEN est_distance_km < 2  THEN '0-2 km'
              WHEN est_distance_km < 5  THEN '2-5 km'
              WHEN est_distance_km < 10 THEN '5-10 km'
              ELSE '10+ km' END                   AS distance_band,
         EXTRACT(HOUR FROM requested_at)          AS hour,
         drop_zone_id,
         quoted_fare,
         fare_at_acceptance - quoted_fare         AS top_up
  FROM ride_requests
  WHERE accepted_at IS NOT NULL
    AND fare_at_acceptance > quoted_fare
    AND requested_at >= DATE '2026-06-01'
    AND requested_at <  DATE '2026-09-01'
)
SELECT city, vehicle_type, distance_band, hour,
       COUNT(*)                                                    AS topped_up_rides,
       PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY top_up)         AS median_top_up,
       ROUND((AVG(top_up / NULLIF(quoted_fare, 0)) * 100)::numeric, 1) AS avg_top_up_pct
FROM nudged
GROUP BY city, vehicle_type, distance_band, hour
HAVING COUNT(*) >= 50
ORDER BY avg_top_up_pct DESC;
3. Cancellations after acceptance: why and how late 
SELECT vehicle_type,
       payment_mode,
       cancel_reason_code,
       COUNT(*) AS cancellations,
       PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (
         ORDER BY EXTRACT(EPOCH FROM (cancelled_at - accepted_at)) / 60) AS median_min_after_accept
FROM ride_requests
WHERE status = 'CANCELLED_BY_CAPTAIN'
  AND accepted_at IS NOT NULL
  AND requested_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 14
GROUP BY vehicle_type, payment_mode, cancel_reason_code
ORDER BY cancellations DESC;
4. Compliance check: pre-ride tip or top-up prompts must be zero 
SELECT DATE(shown_at) AS day,
       prompt_type,
       app_version,
       COUNT(*)       AS times_shown
FROM ui_prompt_events
WHERE prompt_type IN ('PRE_RIDE_TIP', 'FARE_TOP_UP', 'SET_YOUR_PRICE_SLIDER')
  AND ride_state IN ('SEARCHING', 'ACCEPTED', 'IN_TRIP')
  AND shown_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 7
GROUP BY DATE(shown_at), prompt_type, app_version
ORDER BY day, times_shown DESC;
-- Any row after go-live is a bug or an old app version still in use.
5. What customers do on the "still looking" screen 
SELECT city,
       choice,                         -- KEEP_WAITING, SWITCH_VEHICLE, MOVE_PICKUP, CANCEL
       COUNT(*)                        AS customers,
       ROUND(100.0 * AVG(CASE WHEN intent_completed THEN 1 ELSE 0 END), 1) AS went_on_to_ride_pct,
       PERCENTILE_CONT(0.5) WITHIN GROUP (ORDER BY seconds_to_accept_after_choice) AS median_sec_to_accept
FROM still_looking_events
WHERE shown_at >= CURRENT_DATE - 14
GROUP BY city, choice
ORDER BY city, customers DESC;

11. Field research: week one

The data says where requests fail. Only the field says why.

  • Take 20 rides across bike and auto, peak and off-peak, cash and UPI, short and long, and log every acceptance time, captain call and cancellation.
  • Talk to 15 captains at 3 hubs (a metro station, a tech park, a market):
    • When a request comes in, what do you look at first?
    • What makes you skip one? What makes you accept and then cancel?
    • What did a ₹10 or ₹20 top-up actually change for you?
    • Would seeing the drop area and payment mode before accepting change what you do?
  • Talk to 10 customers at metro exits right after a failed or slow booking: what did they do next, and which app did they open?

12. Using AI to make the team faster

  • Read every complaint, not a sample: use an LLM to tag free-text complaints and cancellation notes into a fixed list (extra fare demanded, cash demanded, asked to cancel, wrong pickup, safety). Check it on 200 hand-labelled tickets first, and only trust it if it agrees with people most of the time.
  • Weekly signal: a short weekly summary of what's rising by city and vehicle type, so the team sees a new problem in days, not after a regulator does.

13. Risks I'd watch

  • Upfront prices go up for some trips. Some customers will book less. The fare-per-km guardrail and the switchback test decide whether the trade is worth it.
  • Captain earnings drop if top-ups disappear and nothing replaces them. Watch earnings per online hour; under the subscription model, captains who earn too little stop logging in.
  • Old app versions keep showing banned prompts. Force-update or switch them off server-side; SQL query 4 catches it.
  • "Still looking" claims need data. Saying "captains accept more here" must be backed by real numbers, or it becomes the next dark-pattern finding.

14. What I'd ask in week one

  • What share of completed rides last quarter needed a top-up or tip before acceptance? That's the size of the hole.
  • Which segments needed the biggest top-ups?
  • Do captains already see the drop area and payment mode before accepting, for each vehicle type?
  • What does legal consider a compliant upfront demand price, and how must it be disclosed?
  • How are cancellation penalties and compensation paid today: money or coins?
